03 / WHERE WOULD A BUSINESS SUCCEED?

Quadrant tool: traffic volume vs. business visits, by mode and by category

Every street plotted by its traffic volume (x) against the total visits its storefronts received (y, log scale), split into four quadrants at the subarea median on each axis. This isn't simply "top half good, bottom half bad" -- each quadrant implies a different opportunity: commercial corridor (top-right) is proven, already-working retail/office streets -- safe bets for more of the same. Hidden local draw (top-left) is a street with comparatively little traffic that still pulls real visits -- a sign of underserved demand worth testing with more commercial space. Pass-through (bottom-right) carries a lot of traffic that isn't currently stopping -- the opportunity there is capturing passersby who aren't being given a reason to stop yet, not a street that's already working. Quiet/residential (bottom-left) is the weakest case for new commercial activity either way. Toggle the mode to isolate foot vs. vehicle traffic, switch the y-axis to compare total volume vs. visits per storefront, and highlight a business category to see which quadrant it already clusters in.

Traffic mode

Trip origin x-axis becomes segments with a resolved home location only

Of all measured movement on these streets, a device's home location could only be resolved for 5-16% of segments (the rest -- 84-99% depending on the street -- is labeled "unresolved" in street_traffic_origin.csv, not "pass-through"; SafeGraph simply couldn't match that device to a home). Among segments that WERE resolved, the large majority are local (63-100% depending on the street) and very few are from >3 miles away -- so this subarea sees almost no confirmed regional pass-through traffic, but most trips remain of unknown origin. "Local" and "Distant" below use only the resolved segments, scaled down accordingly -- treat them as a sample, not full volume.

Reading this quadrant

Top-right = high traffic, high visits (proven commercial corridors: Missouri, Montana). Bottom-right = high traffic, low visits (pass-through streets: Ange, Noble, Lee). Top-left = low traffic, high visits (hidden local draws: Nevada). Bottom-left = low traffic, low visits (quiet, largely residential streets).

Source: SafeGraph place polygons and anonymized device-movement data for the Arizona-Rio Grande study polygon, processed with the scripts in /mobility of this repo. Unique visits are unique device-days per place; mode split is derived from device-to-device movement speed and buffered street-centerline proximity (see mobility/README.md for methodology and caveats).
